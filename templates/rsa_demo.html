🛡️ Section 7: How RSA Secures Our Voting System

Confidentiality Analysis

In our electronic voting system, RSA provides confidentiality against eavesdroppers and network snoopers:

  • Asymmetric Security: Voters can encrypt their ballots with the public key (e, n) without needing to share or expose the private key (d, n).
  • Protection of Vote Choices: An adversary monitoring network traffic sees only the cipher integer sequence C, which is mathematically indistinguishable from random noise without d.
  • Computational Hardness: Breaking RSA requires finding prime factors p and q from n. For large bit-lengths, no known polynomial-time classical factoring algorithm exists.

⚠️ Important Practical Distinction: RSA vs. Hybrid Encryption

RSA is computationally expensive because modular exponentiation of very large integers involves intensive CPU operations. Therefore, RSA alone is not standard for encrypting bulk data (such as large files or streams). In production enterprise voting systems, a Hybrid Cryptosystem is utilized:
• RSA (Asymmetric): Securely encrypts a random, lightweight symmetric session key (e.g. AES-256 key).
• AES (Symmetric): Encrypts the actual bulk voting payload with high performance.
